## 0. Signal calibration

The first gate found three signals far outside what their score mapping assumed. Each mapped its z to a score as if z were standard normal (score = P(|Z| < |z|)). Their real z's are much heavier-tailed, so most works scored near 1. Those three now use the empirical counterpart: **score = the share of evaluated works whose statistic is strictly less extreme** (`signals.empirical_tail_score`). The reference population is every evaluated work in the run, both Houses together. Ties share the lower score, so a statistic at its minimum scores 0.

Percentile rank rather than a fitted distribution, per signal:
- **portfolio_concentration** (statistic |z|): z is a share residual whose peer std is floored at 0.02. It is heavy-tailed, with many near-identical values where peers never did the type. No standard family fits both the bulk and the tail.
- **district_authority_pattern** (statistic = the larger of |z_share| and |z_amount|): a maximum of two differently-scaled components, i.e. a mixture with no closed form.
- **temporal_anomaly** (statistic = the one-sided burst z >= 0): built from small integer weekly counts, so it is discrete with a large spike at 0. A continuous fit would misplace the ties.

The other three signals are unchanged: cost anomaly and lifecycle delay keep the normal mapping, near-duplicate keeps cosine similarity. The raw statistics below show why a fitted normal was wrong for the three. For |z| two-sided normal, the median is 0.674 and the P90 is 1.645. For the larger of two such |z|'s, the P90 is about 1.95. For a one-sided z floored at 0, the P90 is about 1.28.
